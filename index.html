How it works

Three months, and then a knock at the door.

Weekends only, so people with jobs and school can still take part. Nobody pays and nobody is paid.

OctFour weekends

Learning

Light, framing, and how to approach a stranger. Taught by photographers who already do this for a living, giving one weekend session each. Cameras are shared and borrowed so you need to bring nothing.

NovThe whole month

Shooting

Ten photographers loose in Arusha. People, streets, work, markets, whatever they find. Everyone shoots as much as they want and posts as they go so that the work is public from day one, not saved for a reveal.

DecFirst three weeks

Choosing and printing

A hundred pictures are selected, printed and mounted. Book the printer for the last week of November

24thChristmas Eve

Giving

A hundred framed pictures carried to a hundred doors including families, small organizations, the people other people put forward. Each one hand-delivered by whoever took it. Nobody posts a picture to a stranger.

Who gets one

Not at random. Chosen, and told why.

Anyone can nominate: a neighbour who feeds people, a teacher who stayed late for years, a mechanic who fixes things for nothing, a small organisation nobody thanks. Every picture goes with a short note naming who put them forward, and what for.

The note is the gift

A beautiful photograph of a town you already live in is decoration. The same photograph, handed over on Christmas Eve with a note explaining that somebody named you, is something people keep on a wall for twenty years. Do not skip the note.

Who decides the hundred

Within November, 10 people will be selected to review nominations and explore the community to pinpoint the hundred that deserve the prints

0 of 100 chosen Delivery day: 24 December
How we photograph people

Nobody is photographed without being asked.

Ten new photographers loose in a town they live in is a responsibility. These are the rules everyone on the project agrees to before they pick up a camera, and they are here in public so anyone can hold us to them.

Asking

We introduce ourselves, say what the project is, and ask before shooting. If someone says no, that is the end of it — no follow-up, no shooting from a distance instead.

Changing your mind

Anyone photographed can ask for their picture to be removed at any point, including after it is printed, and we do it without asking why.

Children

No child is photographed without a parent or guardian present and agreeing. No exceptions, and no pictures of children posted to social media by the students.

Follow the ten

The work is public from the first weekend.

Everything the ten shoot goes up as they shoot it. Two months of consistent posting is worth more to a young photographer here than any certificate, and it is the only thing they are being paid in.

Questions

The things people ask before applying.

Do I need my own camera?

No. Cameras are shared and borrowed for the whole project. If you own one, bring it. If you do not, that is not a reason to stay away — it is closer to the point.

Do I need experience?

No. The whole thing is built for people who have never been taught. If you already shoot professionally, we would rather you applied to teach a weekend than took one of the ten places.

Does it cost anything?

Nothing, at any point. No fee to apply, no fee to attend, no fee to receive a picture. Nobody on the project is paid either — the photographers teaching are giving their weekends.

How old do I have to be?

Old enough to be creative. If you are in school or working, the sessions are on weekends specifically so you can still come.

How much time does it take?

Four weekend sessions across October, then November to shoot at your own pace. If you cannot make most of the October weekends, wait for the next round rather than starting and dropping out — ten places is not many.

Who owns the pictures I take?

You do. The project prints and gives away a hundred of them and posts them publicly with your name on, but the copyright stays with whoever pressed the shutter. Nothing is ever sold.

Can I nominate myself, or my own family?

You can, and people do. Say plainly what the person has done for those around them — that is what the panel reads. Nominating someone is not a guarantee; there are a hundred pictures and there will be more names than that.

What if I am not chosen?

Every name goes on a list for the next round. Everything taught in October gets written up and published here afterwards, free, whether you got a place or not.
