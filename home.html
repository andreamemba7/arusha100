How it works

Three months, and then a knock at the door.

Weekends only, so people with jobs and school can still take part. Nobody pays and nobody is paid.

OctFour weekends

Learning

Light, framing, and how to approach a stranger. Taught by photographers who already do this for a living, giving one weekend session each. Cameras are shared and borrowed so you need to bring nothing.

NovThe whole month

Shooting

Ten photographers loose in Arusha. People, streets, work, markets, whatever they find. Everyone shoots as much as they want and posts as they go so that the work is public from day one, not saved for a reveal.

DecFirst three weeks

Choosing and printing

A hundred pictures are selected, printed and mounted. Book the printer for the last week of November

24thChristmas Eve

Giving

A hundred framed pictures carried to a hundred doors including families, small organizations, the people other people put forward. Each one hand-delivered by whoever took it. Nobody posts a picture to a stranger.

Who gets one

Not at random. Chosen, and told why.

Anyone can nominate: a neighbour who feeds people, a teacher who stayed late for years, a mechanic who fixes things for nothing, a small organisation nobody thanks. Every picture goes with a short note naming who put them forward, and what for.

The note is the gift

A beautiful photograph of a town you already live in is decoration. The same photograph, handed over on Christmas Eve with a note explaining that somebody named you, is something people keep on a wall for twenty years. Do not skip the note.

Who decides the hundred

Within November, 10 people will be selected to review nominations and explore the community to pinpoint the hundred that deserve the prints

0 of 100 chosen Delivery day: 24 December
Follow the ten

The work is public from the first weekend.

Everything the ten shoot goes up as they shoot it. Two months of consistent posting is worth more to a young photographer here than any certificate, and it is the only thing they are being paid in.
